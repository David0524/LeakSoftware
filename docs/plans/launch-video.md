# The Sundeck Pro launch video (plan)

Owner, 2026-10-23: "I need to build a video for this app that hypes it up but also demonstrates
the full functionality. Theme it to be halfway between a SaaS launch and blue collar … blue
collar guys who are running successful businesses. Consider the psychology of men. Show them
that you will make their lives easier, save them time, and show them a vision of a more
successful future. No time limit … Use real screens from the iOS app to show off the tech's uses
and the desktop version to show off the owner's uses. 16:9. Show off all of it."

A new video, built from scratch (owner, 2026-10-23: "Please don't build on top of the other
video. I didn't like it at all"). Nothing is reused from the two 45-second demos
(`demo-videos.md`): not their code, music, look or edit. Only the shop names and the photos and
logos the owner made carry over.

Status: **decided (section 7); building.** The owner generates the narration from the script in
section 8; the music is made in code and cut to it.

## 1. Who it's for, and what moves them

The viewer owns a pool service or leak detection company. He has 2 to 20 trucks, he's good at
the work, and the business has outgrown the way he runs it. He doesn't want "software." He
wants his evenings back, a crew that runs without him on the phone all day, and a company that
looks as good as the work he does.

What the video leans on:

- **Respect first.** He built this. We never call him disorganized, we never tell him he's
  doing it wrong, and we never talk down. The enemy is the paperwork, not him. ("You didn't
  start a business to do paperwork.")
- **Control.** He sees every truck, every job, every dollar from one screen. Nothing happens
  behind his back.
- **Competence and pride.** His reports carry his logo, his quotes look sharp, and customers
  pay before the truck leaves. His company looks like the big outfit in town.
- **Time and money, shown, not claimed.** The app's own numbers do the talking: the route
  optimizer's "−42 min driving", the Dashboard's "Collected" counting up, "Paid" landing
  while the tech is still on site. We don't invent statistics ("save 10 hours a week").
- **The vision.** More trucks, a bigger crew, home for dinner, weekends back. He's building
  something that lasts.
- **Built by people in the trade, for the trade.** Plain words, no buzzwords: no
  "AI-powered", "synergy" or "seamless workflow".

## 2. The look: half launch film, half job site

- **The launch-film half:** big, confident type that slams in word by word. Device shots that
  float and push in on what matters. Hard cuts on the beat. A logo reveal that hits.
- **The job-site half:**
  - Real photos of pools, equipment pads, trucks and hands at work, with a slight film grain.
  - Condensed, heavy, all-business type: Barlow Semi Condensed 800, like a truck door or a
    hard hat sticker.
  - Hard light, concrete and steel textures behind the devices instead of a soft pastel stage.
  - Occasional stencil-style chapter numbers ("01 / DISPATCH").
- **Brand:** navy `#12263A` and water blue `#1F6FAE` carry it. Safety orange `#EE6A1F` is the
  one accent: chapter numbers and the final Start Free Trial. No gradients or glow (BRAND.md).
  The navy/concrete palette already reads "trade", not "startup".
- **Devices:** the tech's side is always the iPhone app (real screens), and the owner's side is
  always the desktop website (real screens). When one action shows up on both, they share the
  frame: a tap on the phone, then the Schedule card turns done on the desktop.
- **Pacing:** the hype parts are fast (cuts every 1 to 2 seconds). The demo parts slow down: one
  idea per shot, each caption on screen at least 2.5 seconds, a tap ring a beat before each
  tap, and push-ins that hold still while you read.

## 3. Shape and length

About **4 minutes**, in 12 chapters. The opening 30 seconds and the closing 30 seconds are pure
hype; the middle is the tour, with a quick hit of energy at every chapter break. Chapters also
make it easy to cut short versions later (a 60-second hype cut, one clip per feature for
social) from the same project.

## 4. Storyboard

Times are approximate; the final cut lands on the music's beats.

### Cold open: the grind (0:00–0:25)
Mostly photos, quick cuts, low drone and a ticking clock.
- "5:12 AM." A truck in a dark driveway, a cooler and a clipboard of paper work orders on the
  seat.
- A whiteboard schedule full of crossed-out names; a phone buzzing with texts ("Is the tech
  coming today?", "Did you get my check?").
- "9:47 PM." The kitchen table: a stack of invoices, a calculator, a cold dinner plate.
- Type, one line at a time: **You're great at the work.** / **The paperwork is killing you.**

### The turn (0:25–0:40)
The music drops in. A hard cut to navy, and the Sundeck Pro logo slams in.
- **Run the whole shop from your truck.**
- A fast montage, one second each: Week board, iPhone Today, a report, a quote accepted, the
  Dashboard.

### 01 / Dispatch: the owner's morning (desktop)
- Schedule › Week: every tech in their own color. A new leak detection lands on Dana's Tuesday.
  Drag a job to Jake.
- Optimize Week: the day maps redraw, and **"−42 min driving"** ticks down.
- Reorder Techs; Today's list in two columns.
- Caption: **Build the week in minutes. Not hours.**

### 02 / On the road: the tech's day (iPhone)
- The Today hero card: the first stop, drive time, access notes ("Gate code 4471, dog in
  yard").
- Tap On My Way: the customer gets a text, and the Lock Screen Live Activity reads "On the way
  · 18 min".
- Swipe from stop to stop; Directions open in Maps.
- Caption: **Your crew knows exactly where to be.**

### 03 / Find the leak (iPhone, leak detection)
- The checklist: pressure test on the return line, marked "Dropped" in red; dye test at the
  light niche.
- Pin the finding on the pool diagram; a photo of the dye pulling into the crack drops in.
- A voice note instead of typing.
- Caption: **Every test. Every finding. On record.**

### 04 / Prove it: the report
- The homeowner report builds page by page with the shop's own logo, photos and findings.
- The customer signs on the tech's phone.
- Send Report: by text and email, before the truck leaves the driveway.
- Caption: **A report that looks like a big company sent it.**

### 05 / Close it on the spot: quotes
- Repair quote from the price book: line items and totals in two taps.
- The customer's phone: Accept Quote, deposit by card. The desktop's Activity shows "Deposit
  paid".
- Caption: **They say yes before you leave.**

### 06 / Every pool, every week: pool service
- Weekly plans fill the Week board for Luis, Priya and Tony.
- iPhone: 12 stops, chlorine and pH readings, and the chemical calculator says what to add.
- The filter cleaning that's due today; before and after photos.
- Mark Complete: the visit report goes to the homeowner on its own.
- Caption: **Your route runs itself.**

### 07 / Spot the upsell
- On a pool visit: a finding (pump noise) becomes a repair quote, and the customer accepts.
- Caption: **Every visit can be a sale.**

### 08 / Get paid
- Monthly invoices for every plan, paid by card.
- Cash or check recorded on the phone, with a receipt sent on the spot.
- Desktop: Invoices, with "Owed to you" shrinking.
- Caption: **Stop chasing checks.**

### 09 / The command center (desktop)
- Dashboard: Collected, Owed to you, Customers, On a plan, Quotes accepted, all counting up.
- The Activity dropdown with the day's events from every truck.
- Customers with counts by filter.
- Caption: **Every truck. Every job. Every dollar. One screen.**

### 10 / Keep them coming back
- Email Customers: a spring opening offer to everyone on a plan, sent to 1,000 customers in
  one click.
- Reminders and review requests go out by themselves; company texting from one number.
- Caption: **Customers who come back. And send their friends.**

### 11 / Built for the backyard
- Quick hits: works with no signal (the sync bar catches up later), one-handed buttons at the
  bottom, big tap targets, dark mode at 6 AM.
- Caption: **No signal? No problem.**

### 12 / The vision (about 3:30–4:00)
Photos again, warm golden-hour light, the music at full lift.
- A row of matching service trucks at sunrise.
- A crew meeting outside a new shop.
- Pulling into the driveway at 5:30 PM; a tailgate at the lake on a Saturday.
- Type, one line at a time: **More trucks.** / **More jobs.** / **Home for dinner.**
- Then: **Build the company you set out to build.**

### End card
The logo, then **Start your free trial**, the only orange button in the video, and sundeckpro.com.

## 8. Narration script

About 590 words: a little over 4 minutes at a steady pace, with room to breathe. One file per numbered line
keeps the edit flexible: name them `01.mp3`, `02.mp3` and so on.

**Voice, in ElevenLabs:** a man in his 40s or 50s, low, calm and certain; he sounds like he's run
crews, not like an announcer. Good starting points in the Voice Library: search "deep narrator",
"documentary" or "gravelly", and pick one without a radio-host polish. Settings: Stability about
50%, Similarity about 75%, Style 0 to 15%, Speaker Boost on. Generate each line two or three times
and keep the take that sounds least like reading. A "..." in the script is a beat of silence;
ElevenLabs reads it as a pause.
