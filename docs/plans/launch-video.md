# The Sundeck Pro launch video (plan)

Owner, 2026-10-23: "I need to build a video for this app that hypes it up but also demonstrates
the full functionality. Theme it to be halfway between a SaaS launch and blue collar … blue
collar guys who are running successful businesses. Consider the psychology of men. Show them
that you will make their lives easier, save them time, and show them a vision of a more
successful future. No time limit … Use real screens from the iOS app to show off the tech's uses
and the desktop version to show off the owner's uses. 16:9. Show off all of it."

A new video, built from scratch (owner, 2026-10-23: "Please don't build on top of the other
video. I didn't like it at all"). Nothing is reused from the two 45-second demos
(`demo-videos.md`): not their code, music, look or edit. Only the shop names and the photos and
logos the owner made carry over.

Status: **decided (section 7); building.** The owner generates the narration from the script in
section 8; the music is made in code and cut to it.

## 1. Who it's for, and what moves them

The viewer owns a pool service or leak detection company. He has 2 to 20 trucks, he's good at
the work, and the business has outgrown the way he runs it. He doesn't want "software." He
wants his evenings back, a crew that runs without him on the phone all day, and a company that
looks as good as the work he does.

What the video leans on:

- **Respect first.** He built this. We never call him disorganized, we never tell him he's
  doing it wrong, and we never talk down. The enemy is the paperwork, not him. ("You didn't
  start a business to do paperwork.")
- **Control.** He sees every truck, every job, every dollar from one screen. Nothing happens
  behind his back.
- **Competence and pride.** His reports carry his logo, his quotes look sharp, and customers
  pay before the truck leaves. His company looks like the big outfit in town.
- **Time and money, shown, not claimed.** The app's own numbers do the talking: the route
  optimizer's "−42 min driving", the Dashboard's "Collected" counting up, "Paid" landing
  while the tech is still on site. We don't invent statistics ("save 10 hours a week").
- **The vision.** More trucks, a bigger crew, home for dinner, weekends back. He's building
  something that lasts.
- **Built by people in the trade, for the trade.** Plain words, no buzzwords: no
  "AI-powered", "synergy" or "seamless workflow".

## 2. The look: half launch film, half job site

- **The launch-film half:** big, confident type that slams in word by word. Device shots that
  float and push in on what matters. Hard cuts on the beat. A logo reveal that hits.
- **The job-site half:**
  - Real photos of pools, equipment pads, trucks and hands at work, with a slight film grain.
  - Condensed, heavy, all-business type: Barlow Semi Condensed 800, like a truck door or a
    hard hat sticker.
  - Hard light, concrete and steel textures behind the devices instead of a soft pastel stage.
  - Occasional stencil-style chapter numbers ("01 / DISPATCH").
- **Brand:** navy `#12263A` and water blue `#1F6FAE` carry it. Safety orange `#EE6A1F` is the
  one accent: chapter numbers and the final Start Free Trial. No gradients or glow (BRAND.md).
  The navy/concrete palette already reads "trade", not "startup".
- **Devices:** the tech's side is always the iPhone app (real screens), and the owner's side is
  always the desktop website (real screens). When one action shows up on both, they share the
  frame: a tap on the phone, then the Schedule card turns done on the desktop.
- **Pacing:** the hype parts are fast (cuts every 1 to 2 seconds). The demo parts slow down: one
  idea per shot, each caption on screen at least 2.5 seconds, a tap ring a beat before each
  tap, and push-ins that hold still while you read.

## 3. Shape and length

About **4 minutes**, in 12 chapters. The opening 30 seconds and the closing 30 seconds are pure
hype; the middle is the tour, with a quick hit of energy at every chapter break. Chapters also
make it easy to cut short versions later (a 60-second hype cut, one clip per feature for
social) from the same project.

## 9. How it's made

Everything is in `apps/video` (package `@leakbook/video`), built from scratch for this film: no
code, music, look or layout from the 45-second videos.

**Tools and licenses.** The frames are plain TypeScript drawing a 1920 × 1080 page, rendered one
frame at a time in headless Chromium through Playwright (`playwright-core`, Apache 2.0) and
encoded with ffmpeg (libx264). Remotion was considered and not used: its license needs a paid
company license for for-profit companies with 4 or more people, and we don't need it. The score is
Python with numpy, synthesized from nothing (no samples or loops). Fonts are the brand's own
Barlow Semi Condensed and Public Sans (OFL) from `brand/fonts`; colors come from
`brand/tokens.json`.

**Make the film** (in `apps/video`; cloud sessions add `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1
PW_CHROMIUM_PATH=/opt/pw-browsers/chromium` to the capture):

1. `node src/capture-web.ts`: the website's real screens from https://sundeckpro.com. Every
   office screen is the Canyon State video shop (`marcus@canyonstate.video.test`: owner Marcus
   Hale, techs Dana Ruiz and Jake Turner), so names, counts and avatars agree, the pool chapter
   included (Canyon State does both services: `cs-week`, Julia Hughes's visit report). The demo company is used
   nowhere. Dispatch (`dispatch-0` to `-4`) is done for real on Canyon State's next week: Laura
   Bennett's new leak detection dragged from Needs a Time onto Tuesday, then to Jake, then
   Optimize Week for everyone and its result. Run `scripts/launch-video-seed.mjs --snapshot-week`
   before it and `--restore-week --apply` after, which puts that week and Needs a Time back. The
   Email Customers offer is typed in but never sent, and the review link is typed into Settings but
   never saved. Rebecca Quinn's report, quote (open, with the terms switch turned on in the
   browser, and accepted) and invoice are her customer pages. In the capture browser only, it
   shows the shops' `.video.test` office addresses as `canyonstateleak.com` and
   `bluemesapools.com` and hides the dashboard's "+N in 30 days" line. A capture that shows
   "demo", "e2e", a test row or `.test` anywhere on screen fails and isn't saved.
2. `node src/capture-iphone.ts <folder>`: copies the iPhone app's real screens: the film's own
   `launch-video-iphone` artifact (`.github/workflows/launch-video-iphone.yml`, suite
   `apps/ios/Tests/SundeckAppScreenshots/LaunchVideoShots.swift`, Rebecca's job #5163). The
   simulator's capture leaves SwiftUI's Canvas blank, so `tools/sign_screen.py` draws her saved
   signature (`assets/owner/rebecca-signature.svg`, the strokes the app saved) into Customer Signs
   the way the app does: aspect-fit, centered, the app's stroke width.
3. `node src/prepare.ts`: times the film to the narration. Each `audio/voice/NN.mp3` (or .wav) is
   measured with ffprobe; a missing line is read at 2.4 words a second. Lines are 0.6 s apart,
   1.0 s before a chapter (its card plays there, about 1.2 to 1.6 s with the beat; the vision has
   no card), 1.2 s of silence before "Not anymore.", and 0.5 s extra after lines 19, 29 and 33.
   The hit lands just after "Not anymore."; after it every line starts on the music's beat
   (92 BPM). The end card holds about 5 seconds. Writes `build/timeline.json`.
4. `python3 music/launch_score.py`: the score, built on that timeline (drone and clock, the hit,
   the groove, a fill and crash into every chapter card, the vision's swell, the end hit). It
   steps back a quarter second before each word and stays down through a line's pauses; the
   vision comes up about 5 dB between lines and goes further down under the voice. Writes
   `build/mix.wav`: the voice at about -16 LUFS, the music at -29 LUFS integrated under it, the
   whole at -14 LUFS with true peaks under -1 dBFS. `python3 music/check_mix.py [film.mp4]`
   measures the film's loudness and true peak, and for every line the music under the voice as
   heard: short-term loudness (3 s windows; momentary for a line under 3 s), the smallest gap
   while the voice speaks. It fails when any line's music is less than 10 dB under.
5. `node src/render.ts`: the film, `out/sundeck-pro-launch.mp4` (1920 × 1080, 60 fps, H.264,
   AAC), in 4 parallel segments (`--workers`). Every frame waits for its images to decode, so a
   scene never opens on a blank frame. `--stills 12.5,40` writes single frames to
   `build/stills/` to look at; `--from 30 --to 60` renders a stretch.

  takes re-time everything. Captions: one key line per chapter, in with its line and held at
